FONTLOG for the Appli-Tec family
================================

Basic Font Information
----------------------
Appli-Tec is the corporate typeface of Applitec Moutier SA, used for technical
documentation: product catalogues, data sheets, office documents and the web.

It is a derivative of D-DIN, copyright (c) 2017 Datto Inc., released by Datto
under the SIL Open Font License v1.1. D-DIN was designed by Charles Nix
(Monotype) and is itself an adaptation of DIN 1451, the typeface defined by the
Deutsches Institut fuer Normung in 1931 for industrial and traffic signage.

D-DIN ships with two weights only (Regular 400 and Bold 700). Appli-Tec extends
the family with the intermediate and extreme weights required by Applitec's
documents. The additional weights are computed from the two original drawings
by point-to-point interpolation and extrapolation; no outline was redrawn from
scratch.

As required by clause 3 of the SIL Open Font License, this derivative does NOT
use the Reserved Font Name "D-DIN". "Appli-Tec" is reserved in turn.

Coverage: Basic Latin, Latin-1 Supplement, plus the ISO 8859-15 additions.
This covers French, German, English, Italian and Spanish in full.
It does NOT cover Central European, Turkish, Greek or Cyrillic.

Weights
-------
  Light     300    generated (interpolation factor -0.333)
  Regular   400    original drawing
  Medium    500    generated (+0.333)
  Bold      700    original drawing
  Heavy     800    generated (+1.333, extrapolated)
  Black     900    generated (+1.667, extrapolated - see ChangeLog)

Fixes applied to defects inherited from D-DIN
---------------------------------------------
- "periodcentered" (U+00B7) is an EMPTY glyph in D-DIN Bold. Rebuilt from the
  period glyph.
- Zero-area stray contours in "dollar", "colon" and "semicolon" removed.
- "dollar" and "cent" have different contour topology between Regular and Bold
  (counters merge in the Bold). Counters are now closed progressively.

Known limitations
-----------------
- Kerning inherited from D-DIN is very sparse (22-45 pairs per weight).
  A proper kerning pass is still outstanding.
- Black 900 closes the counters more than is comfortable (74.3% ink coverage
  in the "e", against a 72% working threshold). Suitable for display sizes;
  needs manual correction before use at text sizes. Heavy 800 is the safe
  alternative and is supplied alongside for comparison.
