FONTLOG for the Appli-Tec family
================================

Basic Font Information
----------------------
Appli-Tec is the corporate typeface of Applitec Moutier SA, used for technical
documentation: product catalogues, data sheets, office documents and the web.

It is a derivative of D-DIN, copyright (c) 2017 Datto Inc., released by Datto
under the SIL Open Font License v1.1. D-DIN was designed by Charles Nix
(Monotype) and is itself an adaptation of DIN 1451, the typeface defined by the
Deutsches Institut fuer Normung in 1931 for industrial and traffic signage.

D-DIN ships with two weights only (Regular 400 and Bold 700). Appli-Tec extends
the family with the intermediate and extreme weights required by Applitec's
documents. The additional weights are computed from the two original drawings
by point-to-point interpolation and extrapolation; no outline was redrawn from
scratch.

As required by clause 3 of the SIL Open Font License, this derivative does NOT
use the Reserved Font Name "D-DIN". "Appli-Tec" is reserved in turn.

Coverage: Basic Latin, Latin-1 Supplement, plus the ISO 8859-15 additions.
This covers French, German, English, Italian and Spanish in full.
It does NOT cover Central European, Turkish, Greek or Cyrillic.

Styles
------
Five weights, each with a matching italic: 10 files in the normal width.

OpenType naming follows the Windows four-style limit: the RIBBI set (Regular,
Italic, Bold, Bold Italic) sits under the family "Appli-Tec", while Light,
Medium and Heavy form their own Windows sub-families ("Appli-Tec Light" etc.).
Name IDs 16/17 carry the typographic family so that modern applications group
all ten files under a single "Appli-Tec".

Weights
-------
  Light     300    generated (interpolation factor -0.333)
  Regular   400    original drawing
  Medium    500    generated (+0.333)
  Bold      700    original drawing
  Heavy     800    generated (+1.333, extrapolated) - retained display weight

Black 900 was evaluated and NOT retained: it closes the counters too far
(74.3% ink coverage in the "e", against a 72% working threshold). It can be
regenerated at any time with:  python3 tools/build.py 1.6667 900 Black

Italics
-------
D-DIN ships ONE italic, in the Regular weight. The other four are derived by
transferring the weight delta measured on the upright axis:

    italic(w) = italic(400) + shear( upright(w) - upright(400) )

with shear = tan(12 deg) = 0.21256, the exact slant of the original italic
(measured at 0.2130 on I, H and 1; the designer sheared about y = 254.1).

This matters: the D-DIN italic is NOT a mechanical oblique. Of 169 comparable
simple glyphs, 64 were redrawn after slanting - the round ones above all
(a, e, g, s, 0, 2, 3). The italic "a" is a single-storey form, a different
letter from the upright's two-storey "a". Slanting the uprights would have
discarded that work. Transferring the delta preserves it: only the thickness
changes.

Two traps had to be dealt with.

FIRST: slanting a shape DESTROYS its horizontal extrema. A point with a
vertical tangent is no longer vertical after a 12-degree shear, so an italic
and its upright do not expose the same structural features - the "e" shows 13
on one side and 10 on the other, and nothing lines up. Matching is therefore
done on the UNSHEARED italic, and the result is re-slanted at the very end.

SECOND: micro-features. The terminal of the upright "e" carries two vertical
stubs 3 and 5 units long, artefacts of the drawing rather than articulations
of the letter; the italic, whose terminal is cut at a different angle, has
none. Two things went wrong there. Features closer together than 15 units are
now merged, a corner winning over an extremum. And when a span needs extra
points to match the other master, they are inserted AT THE POSITION THEY
OCCUPY in that master, no longer by splitting the longest segment in half -
which used to drop them at the bottom of the curve, 120 units from where they
belonged, and let the terminal's weight delta carve a notch. Residuals: "e"
123 -> 29 units, "c" 129 -> 28, "s" 127 -> 24.

Nine glyphs are sheared from the upright at the target weight rather than
derived from the italic: a, dollar, cent, bar, brokenbar, ordfeminine,
braceright, perthousand, .notdef. For dollar and cent the counters merge in
the upright Bold, which leaves the delta undefined. For the others - "a"
above all, whose italic is a single-storey letter against the upright's
two-storey one - no correspondence exists at all.

For "a" this is a deliberate, provisional choice by the client: a shear now,
a proper redrawing of the single-storey italic form later. It follows that
Appli-Tec Italic 400 differs from the original D-DIN Italic on these nine
glyphs, and matches it to within 1.01 units on all the others.

tools/epaissir.py remains in the tree, unwired. It thickens an outline
without any reference master, and was measured at about 27 units of deviation
against the real Monotype Bold (against about 1 unit for true interpolation).
It is the starting point should the single-storey italic "a" be taken up.

Fixes applied to defects inherited from D-DIN
---------------------------------------------
- "periodcentered" (U+00B7) is an EMPTY glyph in D-DIN Bold. Rebuilt from the
  period glyph.
- Zero-area stray contours in "dollar", "colon" and "semicolon" removed.
- "dollar" and "cent" have different contour topology between Regular and Bold
  (counters merge in the Bold). Counters are now closed progressively.

Known limitations
-----------------
- Kerning inherited from D-DIN is very sparse (22-45 pairs per weight).
  A proper kerning pass is still outstanding.
- Heavy 800 is the heaviest retained weight (70.6% ink coverage in the "e",
  within the 72% working threshold).

ChangeLog
---------
2026-09-18  v1.004  Fixed the notch reported on the italic e, c and s:
                    micro-features were being matched across designs, and
                    filler points were inserted by halving the longest
                    segment instead of at their corresponding position.
                    The italic "a" now follows the upright, sheared, by
                    client decision.
                    Verified: uprights still reproduce D-DIN Regular to
                    within 1.00 unit; italics reproduce D-DIN Italic to
                    within 1.01 units on the 81 glyphs not sheared.

2026-09-18  v1.003  Fixed italic distortion reported on e, s and a. Two
                    causes, see the Italics section above: features matched
                    across a shear, and seven glyphs that are different
                    letters in the two styles.
                    Verified: x-height dispersion between a/e/o/s down to
                    1 unit at every weight; italic stems still match their
                    uprights to within 0.3 units; t=0 still reproduces the
                    original D-DIN Italic to within 1.01 units.

2026-09-18  v1.002  Added the five italics (Light, Regular, Medium, Bold,
                    Heavy) by weight-delta transfer onto the original italic.
                    Rewrote OpenType naming: style bits now follow the
                    sub-family rather than the absolute weight - Heavy 800 was
                    declaring itself Bold, so Word would have offered it as
                    the bold of its own family. Name ID 2 was also carrying
                    "Light"/"Medium"/"Heavy" where Windows requires one of
                    Regular/Italic/Bold/Bold Italic.
                    Verified: the regenerated Italic reproduces the original
                    D-DIN Italic to within 1.01 units; italic stems match
                    their uprights to within 0.3 units at every weight.

2026-09-18  v1.001  Fixed outline distortion affecting 33 glyphs (dents in
                    the bowls of p, d, g; broken curves on h, m, 2, 5, 9).
                    Root cause: points of the two masters were paired by
                    distance travelled along the contour, which is not a
                    stable reference across weights - a point on the bowl of
                    the "p" could be paired with a point elsewhere in the
                    letter. Pairing is now anchored on structural features
                    (corners and extrema), which are stable across weights,
                    and contours are matched to each other before comparison.
                    Verified: the regenerated Regular reproduces the original
                    D-DIN Regular to within 1 unit (integer rounding).
                    Black 900 dropped in favour of Heavy 800.

2026-09-18  v1.000  Step 1. Upright weights in the normal width.
